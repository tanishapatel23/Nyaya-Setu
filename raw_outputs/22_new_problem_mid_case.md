The law, in plain words
[SAL-1] Wages you have earned cannot be held back
    Salary for days you already worked is yours. The Code on Wages fixes when wages must be paid, and an employer cannot sit on earned wages because of a pending clearance or a disagreement about handover. At most they can adjust what your contract actually allows, such as a notice period shortfall, and they should show you the calculation.
    Source: Code on Wages, 2019, Section 17
[SAL-2] You can file a wage claim within three years
    If a written demand does not work, you can file a claim with the labour authority instead of going to a civil court, online through the SAMADHAN portal or at the Labour Commissioner's office. The claim can be filed within three years, and the authority can order payment plus compensation.
    Source: Code on Wages, 2019, Section 45
[SAL-3] Final settlement is supposed to be quick
    The Code on Wages expects full and final dues within two working days when someone resigns or is removed. Many companies still run 30 to 45 day cycles, and whether this applies to you can depend on your role, so use it as pressure in your demand rather than as a guarantee.
    Source: Code on Wages, 2019, Section 17
