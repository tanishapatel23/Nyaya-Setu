> I got a legal notice saying my cheque of 50000 bounced and I must pay within 15 days. It came 2 days ago.

**NyaySetu (answer):**

```
! A notice about a bounced cheque is more serious than an ordinary demand. Under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, the person who issued the cheque usually has 15 days from receiving the notice to pay before a criminal complaint can be filed. Talk to a lawyer today.

You received a legal notice 2 days ago (around 8 Nov 2026) asking for Rs 50,000. A notice is not a court case. It is usually the step before one, so it deserves a calm written reply before the deadline.

Time limit: Reply to the notice by 23 Nov 2026, 13 days from now.

What to do
1. Today: Write down the date you got it and keep the envelope. Not replying at all is the worst option.
2. Before 23 Nov 2026: Send a written reply by registered post. A draft is in the Documents tab. Do not admit anything that is not true.
3. If needed: If it involves a cheque, property, the police or a court date, talk to a lawyer before replying. 15100 is free.

Your proof
   [get]  the notice and its envelope (the postmark shows when your reply clock started)
   [get]  your own records of the deal (agreements, receipts or chats that support your side)

The law, in plain words
[NOT-1] A legal notice is not a court case, but do not ignore it
    A legal notice is a formal letter, usually sent through a lawyer, saying what the other side wants and by when. It does not mean a case has been filed. But it is often the step before one, and if you stay silent they can later show the court that you never disputed it.
    Source: General practice
[NOT-2] Reply in writing, on the facts, before the deadline
    The usual response is a calm written reply, sent before the deadline by registered post, that answers their points one by one and gives your side. Do not admit things that are not true, and do not threaten them. If the notice is about a cheque, a property, or anything involving the police or a court date, get a lawyer to write the reply.
    Source: General practice

NyaySetu gives general legal information, not legal advice. It is not a lawyer. For anything serious, urgent or already in court, talk to an advocate. Free legal aid is available on the NALSA helpline 15100.
```
