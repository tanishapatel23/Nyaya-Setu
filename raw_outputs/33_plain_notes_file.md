The law, in plain words
[DEP-1] The deposit has to come back once you hand over the house
    A security deposit is your money. The landlord can keep part of it only for unpaid rent or for damage beyond normal wear and tear, and he should be able to show what he deducted and why. Once you vacate and hand back the keys, the rest is due to you. The Model Tenancy Act also caps the deposit for a home at two months' rent.
    Source: Model Tenancy Act, 2021, Section 11
[DEP-2] Start with a written demand, then go to the Rent Authority or a civil court
    A written demand with a clear deadline, usually 15 days, settles a lot of these cases and makes the later case stronger. If your State has set up a Rent Authority under the Model Tenancy Act, that is where the dispute goes. If not, you file a civil suit to recover the money. A consumer commission usually will not hear a deposit dispute.
    Source: Model Tenancy Act, 2021, Rent Authority and Rent Court
